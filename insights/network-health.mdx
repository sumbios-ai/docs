Network Health gives you four separate measures of your network and one suggestion of where to put your attention. It is a starting point for reflection, not a grade for you or a score for any person.

## When you would use it

After a large import, or before a week of meetings. You want to know whether too much of your access runs through one or two people, whether O knows enough about your connections to help you, and which relationship deserves an hour this week.

## Where to find it

Select **Insights** in the sidebar, then the **Network Health** tab. The address is `/intelligence/network-health`.

| # | Element | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Relationship goals** | How many direct connections O counts, and whether you have set a personal goal. **Edit relationship goals** opens Settings → **Goals**. |
| 2 | **Updated** and **Refresh** | When O last calculated the page. **Refresh** calculates it again, for example after an import. |
| 3 | **Relationship to strengthen** | One relationship that a large share of your measured access runs through, with how many measured paths it connects and across how many industry groups. |
| 4 | **Measured access** | Says what the suggestion is based on: paths O can measure in the data you have connected. |
| 5 | **Strengthen this relationship** and **Find another path** | **Strengthen this relationship** opens [Field Mode](/getting-started/field-mode) so you can add context about that person. **Find another path** opens [Connectors](/insights/connectors) on that person, to look for a second route. |
| 6 | **Health by factor** | The four measures at a glance. Each stands on its own; O does not average them into a single grade. |

| # | Element | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Network Reach** | Your progress towards a personal connection goal. |
| 2 | **Context Coverage** | How much useful context O has for introductions, reminders and recommendations. |
| 3 | **Connection Paths** | The paths O can see beyond your direct network. An inventory, not a score. |
| 4 | **Network Breadth** | How varied your network is across industries, countries and seniority levels. |
| 5 | Status | A word for where the factor stands, such as **Strong**, **Partial view** or **Goal required**. |
| 6 | **How this is calculated** | The method and the data it uses. Close it with **×**. |
| 7 | Action | The next step for that factor: **Set a personal target**, **Review recent connections**, **Reveal more paths** or **Review network strengths**. |

### What each factor measures

| Factor | How O calculates it | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Network Reach** | Compares your direct connections with your personal target. Until you set one, the card reads **Goal required**. | Confirmed direct connections and similar-role network sizes |
| **Context Coverage** | Checks eight useful profile and relationship details for each direct connection, then reports the percentage available. | Connected profiles and your linked notes |
| **Connection Paths** | Lists active **O friends**, **Observed mutual links**, stored **2nd-degree people** and **3rd-degree people**, and **Inferred hidden bridges**. The categories may overlap and are never combined into a percentage. | O friendships, the selected network graph, and hidden-bridge signals |
| **Network Breadth** | Scores **Industry breadth**, **Country breadth** and **Seniority breadth** higher when your connections are spread more widely, then averages the three. **Known industries** counts the industries O has seen. | Current industry, country and seniority details |

**Yours vs ICP peer avg.** on Network Reach compares the size of your network with the average for people in roles like yours. It is shown beside the score and never changes it.

**People with context notes** on Context Coverage counts the people you have written notes about. The eight details also include profile information, so the two numbers can differ.

## Add context while it is fresh

O lists people you connected with recently and asks how you know them. Choose **Add context** to write it down while you still remember; it is saved as a note on that person.

## Where your network is strongest

The industries, locations, seniority levels and skills most represented in your network. Each tile gives the top value and its share of the connections for which O knows that detail. Below it, **Key connectors to nurture** lists people a lot of your measured access runs through; **Key connectors** opens the full list in [Connectors](/insights/connectors).

## How to

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set a personal reach target">
    Choose **Edit relationship goals**, or **Set a personal target** on the Network Reach card. In Settings → **Goals**, enter your target. Network Reach then shows your progress against it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give O more context">
    Choose **Review recent connections** on Context Coverage, or go to **Add context while it is fresh**. Choose **Add context** on a person and write how you know them. Notes are one of the details Context Coverage checks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reveal more paths">
    On Connection Paths, choose **Reveal more paths**. With the O browser extension, open a LinkedIn profile and use **Warm Path**: it adds the mutual, second-degree and third-degree relationships it observes to O.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Act on a suggestion">
    Under **Relationship to strengthen** or **Your next best moves**, open a suggestion. Decide for yourself whether to reach out; O never contacts anyone for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refresh after a change">
    After an import or a newly connected source, choose **Refresh** so the measures use the new data.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What O does behind it

- **Four independent measures.** Each score stands on its own. O does not combine them into one number.
- **Missing is not zero.** Missing data is shown as unavailable rather than scored as zero. A zero means O observed none in an available source; a dash means that source was unavailable.
- **Paths are an inventory.** Connection Paths lists what O can see, in categories that may overlap and are never added into a total.
- **Only what you connected.** O measures only the information you have connected or imported. The method is also on the page, under **Data coverage and methodology**.

<Note>
**A measure, not a verdict.** A missing result may mean there is not enough information. It does not mean a relationship does not exist, and nothing on this page scores a person.
</Note>
