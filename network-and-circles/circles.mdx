A Circle holds the people and companies you keep together for one purpose, such as a deal, a project, a hiring search or a shared interest. Inside it, O puts what you know about those people next to what needs a follow-up.

## When you would use it

You are working on something that involves a dozen people across a few companies. Instead of keeping them in your head or in a spreadsheet, you put them in a Circle. Each time you open it, you see who has notes, who has a reminder due and what has changed.

## Where to find it

| 5 | Actions (**···**) | **Open**, **Edit**, **Discuss with friends** and **Delete** for a Circle you own. |

## Inside a Circle

Open a Circle to see its **Overview**. The buttons beside the title switch between four views: **Overview**, **People** (every member, with search, **Filter** and **Export**), **Map** (how the members connect) and **Signals** (recent changes for the Circle's members).

| # | Element | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Views | **Overview**, **People**, **Map** and **Signals**. |
| 2 | **Feed settings** | Chooses which sections are turned on for this Circle: **News**, **Activity** and **Company Signals**. |
| 3 | **Import CSV/XLSX** | Adds people to the Circle from a spreadsheet. |
| 4 | Actions (**···**) | **Edit Details**, **Export CSV** and **Delete Circle**. See [Export or delete a Circle](/network-and-circles/export-or-delete-a-circle). |
| 5 | **Circle intelligence** | O gathers what you have saved, your follow-ups and recent signals for the Circle. When information is missing, O shows that it is missing instead of guessing. |
| 6 | **Ask O what matters**, **View people**, **Open Notes**, **Review signals** | Ask O about this Circle, or jump to its people, its notes or its signals. |
| 7 | **Relationship context** | The share of members that have at least one note, reminder or recent signal in O. It does not mean O knows your whole history with them. |
| 8 | Counters | **Members** in the Circle, **Saved context** (members with notes), **Follow-ups** (reminders not done yet) and **Recent signals** (recent changes O can see). |
| 9 | **Follow up next** | Reminders not done yet come first, then members with recent signals. |
| 10 | **Where this Circle works** | The companies the members work at. Beside it, **Where relationships are** shows their locations. |

## How to

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a Circle">
    Choose **Create Circle**, give it a name that says what it is for, and add people. You can also ask O in your own words, or use `/add-to-circle` in [Ask O](/getting-started/home).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add people from a spreadsheet">
    Open the Circle and choose **Import CSV/XLSX**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See what needs attention">
    Open the Circle's **Overview**. Start with **Follow up next**, then look at **Relationship context** to see which members O knows little about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build context">
    Choose **Open Notes** to write what you know, or set a reminder for a member. Both count towards **Relationship context**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow changes">
    In **Feed settings**, turn on the sections you want, then check **Signals** for recent changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask about the group">
    Choose **Ask O what matters** to ask about this Circle in Ask O.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What O does behind it

- **A Circle is yours.** It is your way of organising people. It does not mean they have joined a group together, and they are not told.
- **Private until you share it.** A lock in the list marks a private Circle. Circles shared with you show who shared them under **Shared by**. Friends see your Circles only if you turn on **Circles** in their [access settings](/network-and-circles/friends-and-sharing).
- **Evidence, not guesses.** What O knows about the members, and the follow-ups it shows, come from your notes, your reminders and the signals O can see. Where O has nothing, the page says so.

<Note>
**Exports contain people's details.** **Export CSV** downloads member information. Keep the file somewhere safe, and delete it when you no longer need it.
</Note>
